# JDW Landscapes — Call Cheat Sheet

## Core message

James does **not** need to jump straight to two paid leads a day.

The smarter plan is to:

1. fix missed-call recovery, review collection and tracking;
2. prepare Google Local Services Ads in parallel;
3. start with **£500/month media spend**;
4. measure the real CPL and customer acquisition cost;
5. scale only when the economics work.

## Key numbers to explain

- 2 leads/day ≈ **60 leads/month**
- At £35–£70 CPL, 60 leads could require about **£2,100–£4,200/month** in ad spend
- James said JDW can handle **5+ extra jobs/month**
- At a planning 20–35% lead-to-customer rate, around **15–25 leads/month** may be enough to create 5 customers
- Recommended starting media spend: **£500/month**
- Initial planning output at £500: roughly **7–14 leads**
- First month total: **£697**
- From month 2 at £500 media spend: **£897/month**

## Call flow

### 1. Start with his target

“You mentioned wanting around two leads a day. I’ve looked at the numbers properly and I actually don’t think you need to pay for two leads a day yet.”

### 2. Translate it

“Two a day is about 60 leads a month. At the sort of lead costs we need to plan around, that could eventually mean £2,000–£4,000+ a month in ad spend.”

### 3. Bring it back to capacity

“You said you can take at least five extra jobs a month. So the goal is really to buy enough decent enquiries to create those five jobs, not to buy leads for the sake of it.”

### 4. Recommend the first test

“I’d start at around £500 in ad spend. That lets us find out what a JDW lead actually costs and how many turn into quotes and jobs before we start increasing the budget.”

### 5. Explain the foundations

“Your website is already decent. I wouldn’t rebuild it for the sake of it. The main things I’d fix are missed-call recovery, follow-up, review collection and proper tracking.”

### 6. Missed-call point

“If we’re paying £40, £50 or £60 for somebody to ring you and you’re on a job and miss it, I don’t want that lead just disappearing. We can instantly text them back, get the job details and put them into the system.”

### 7. Explain tracking simply

“We’ll be able to see lead → quote → job. So rather than me telling you the ads are working because we got ten leads, we can see whether they actually made you money.”

### 8. Channel recommendation

“I’d test Google Local Services Ads first because those people are already looking for a landscaper. Meta is something we can test later because your work is very visual.”

### 9. Explain the first-month deal

“Normally the system and ad management together are £397 before the ad spend. I’m waiving the £200 ad-management fee for month one, so with £500 of advertising your first month is £697. If we keep the same ad budget, it’s £897 from month two.”

### 10. Close

“What I’d do is get the lead system sorted now, prepare the Google ads at the same time and start with the £500 test. Give us enough time to get real numbers, then scale only if those enquiries are turning into profitable jobs. Does that route make sense to you?”

Then stop talking and let James answer.

## If he asks for guarantees

“I wouldn’t give you a fake guarantee on lead or job numbers. We’re modelling roughly £35–£70 per enquiry and 20–35% converting into customers, but the purpose of the first campaign is to replace those assumptions with your real numbers.”

## Best line

“You said you wanted two leads a day, but I actually don’t think you need to pay for two leads a day yet. The smarter target is enough leads to fill the extra jobs you can actually handle.”
